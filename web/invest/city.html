    How to read this

    Each hexagon is an area of about 5 km². Its score, from 0 to 100, combines highway, rail and metro access, the main-road network and built-up growth since 2000, weighted by the preset you choose.

    Growth favours the fringe, access favours the core. The highest scores often sit in the middle ring, between an established centre and fast-growing edges.

    When a factor could not be measured in an area it is left out of that area's score, never counted as zero, and shows as a dash.

    Faded hexagons have too few residents to rank. They stay on the map but not in the list.

    Bus stops are shown where a city publishes a bus feed. They are not part of any score, so cities with and without a feed stay comparable.

    We measure road network capacity, not pavement condition.
